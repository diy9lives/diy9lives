Quick answers

How do I read a tire size like 225/60R16?

225 is the tread width in millimeters. 60 is the aspect ratio — the sidewall height as a percentage of the width (here, 60% of 225mm = 135mm tall). R16 means it fits a 16-inch wheel. Overall diameter = wheel + two sidewalls.

Why does tire size affect my speedometer?

Your speedometer counts wheel rotations and assumes the stock tire circumference. A larger tire covers more ground per rotation, so your speedometer reads lower than your true speed. A smaller tire does the opposite. Even 2–3% matters: at a true 70 mph that's about 2 mph of error — enough to earn a ticket or fail a state inspection in some places.

What is "plus sizing"?

Going up one inch in wheel diameter while keeping the overall tire diameter nearly the same. The pattern: +1 inch of wheel ≈ +10mm width and −5 to −10 aspect ratio. Example: 225/60R16 → 225/55R17 → 245/45R18 → 245/40R19. Bigger wheel, shorter sidewall, same rollout.

Will a wider tire fit my car?

This calculator matches diameters, but width clearance depends on your specific wheel offset, suspension, and fender room. As a rule, +10 to +20mm over stock is usually fine; beyond that, check a fitment forum for your exact model or measure clearance at full steering lock.

Why don't I see every mathematically close size?

Tire makers don't produce every possible width/aspect-ratio combination — only specific sizes get manufactured and stocked at scale. This tool matches your stock diameter against sizes that are actually common in retailer catalogs, so what you see here is realistically shoppable. Even so, exact in-stock availability and pricing shift often, so it's worth checking a couple of sellers before you commit to a size.
