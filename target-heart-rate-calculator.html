How it works

Maximum heart rate is estimated as 220 minus your age. The five zones are percentages of that number: 50–60%, 60–70%, 70–80%, 80–90%, and 90–100%. If you enter a resting heart rate, the tool switches to the Karvonen method, where each target is (maximum − resting) × intensity + resting, which accounts for your fitness level.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is 220 minus age?

It is a rough average that can be off by 10 to 15 beats per minute for an individual. A supervised stress test is the only way to find your true maximum.

How do I measure my resting heart rate?

Check your pulse first thing in the morning before getting out of bed, counting beats for 60 seconds, ideally over a few days.

What is Zone 2?

The 60–70% zone, where you can still hold a conversation. It is widely used for building aerobic base and endurance.

Do medications affect these numbers?

Yes. Some medications, including beta blockers, lower heart rate. If you take medication or have a heart condition, ask your doctor for zones that suit you.
