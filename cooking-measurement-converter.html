Why ingredient matters
⚖
1 cup of flour weighs about 120g, but 1 cup of honey weighs about 340g. Volume converters that ignore this give you wrong weights for anything except water.
📝
How you measure flour changes its weight — spooned-and-leveled flour is lighter than scooped-and-packed. This tool assumes the spooned method, standard in most modern recipes.
⚠
These are standard approximations, not brand-specific. If a recipe gives both a volume and a gram weight, trust the gram weight — it's always more precise.

How it works

Volume units (cups, tablespoons, teaspoons, fluid ounces, milliliters, liters) are converted to milliliters and weight units (grams, kilograms, ounces, pounds) to grams. Converting between volume and weight needs the ingredient's density, so you pick one, such as flour at about 120 grams per cup, granulated sugar at about 200, butter at about 227, or honey at about 340. Same-type conversions, like cups to tablespoons, do not depend on the ingredient. Cups are US cups of 236.588 milliliters.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I need to pick an ingredient?

Because a cup of flour and a cup of honey have very different weights. Density turns volume into weight correctly.

Why does flour weight vary?

Scooping packs flour more tightly than spooning and leveling. This tool assumes the spooned and leveled method, which most modern recipes use.

Should I trust the gram weight or the cup amount in a recipe?

Trust grams. Weight is more precise than volume, so if a recipe gives both, follow the gram figure.

Are these densities exact?

They are standard approximations. Brands, humidity, and packing can shift them a little.
