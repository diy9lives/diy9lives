How it works

The tool estimates your basal metabolic rate with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation: 10 × weight in kg + 6.25 × height in cm − 5 × age, then +5 for men or −161 for women. That is multiplied by an activity factor (1.2 sedentary, 1.375 light, 1.55 moderate, 1.725 active, 1.9 very active) to get your daily maintenance calories (TDEE). Goals adjust it using the common estimate that about 3,500 calories equals one pound, so a half-pound-a-week change is roughly 250 calories a day. Macro targets use 0.8 g of protein per pound of body weight, 25% of calories from fat, and the rest from carbohydrates.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is this estimate?

Equations like Mifflin-St Jeor are good starting points but can be off by a few hundred calories for any one person. Track your weight for a few weeks and adjust up or down based on what actually happens.

Is the 3,500 calories per pound rule exact?

It is a useful approximation, not a law. Real weight change varies with water, muscle, and metabolic adaptation, so progress is rarely perfectly linear.

Why should I recalculate as I lose weight?

A lighter body burns fewer calories, so your maintenance level drops over time. Recalculating every 10 to 15 pounds keeps the target realistic.

Is it safe to eat very few calories?

Very low calorie intakes should only be done under medical supervision. If you have a health condition, are pregnant, or have a history of disordered eating, talk to a doctor or registered dietitian first.
