How the tools are built and reviewed

DIY9lives is run by David Liu. The point of this page is to make the process checkable: what is calculated, where the inputs come from, how examples are tested, and what happens when something is wrong.

What gets published

A tool is published when it does a specific job that can be described in one sentence, runs without an account, and produces a result a reader can reproduce by hand. A guide is published when it connects tools to a real decision and includes at least one worked example with actual numbers.

Pages that are only a form with no explanation, or that duplicate a better page, are not kept in the index. The site would rather have forty useful pages than ninety thin ones.

How a calculator is built

  1. Write the formula first, in plain notation, before any interface work. If the formula cannot be explained on the page, the tool is not ready.
  2. Pick the smallest set of inputs that changes the answer. Defaults are realistic starting values, not marketing numbers.
  3. Show the result and the intermediate values that explain it, so a reader can see why the answer moved.
  4. Run the tool with at least three inputs: a typical case, a boundary case, and a case where the formula should fail or need a warning.
  5. Compare the result with an independent hand calculation or with the cited source's own example.
  6. Check the page at phone width and confirm the result is still readable without horizontal scrolling.

Where the numbers come from

Anything that is not arithmetic on the reader's own inputs is cited on the page where it is used. Preference order:

  • Government agencies and official statistics (IRS, CFPB, NHTSA, CDC, NIH, USDA, EIA, EPA, SSA, state revenue departments).
  • Standards bodies and technical references (NIST, SAE, ASTM, ACI, ASHRAE, ETRTO where applicable).
  • Manufacturer instructions and published coverage or yield data (paint, concrete, insulation, tires).
  • Peer-reviewed or clinical guidance for health topics (ACOG, WHO, Dietary Guidelines).

Assumptions that belong to this site, such as a waste allowance or a default door area, are labelled as assumptions rather than presented as facts. Statistics are not invented, rounded up for effect, or attributed to a source that does not say them.

Worked examples and dates

Every worked example is recalculated from the same formula the tool uses. If the example and the tool disagree, the page is wrong and gets fixed. The review date at the bottom of a page changes when the content or the formula changes, not on a publishing schedule.

ChangeWhat the date means
Formula or input changedNew review date, and the example is recalculated.
Source updatedNew review date, with the old source replaced or noted.
Typo or link fixDate may stay the same; the correction is still made.
No changeNo new date. Dates are not refreshed to look active.

Corrections

Send the page, the input you used, what you expected and what you saw through the contact page. A reproducible error gets a fix and a dated note. If a reader points to a better source, the source list is updated even when the number does not change.

The site does not delete a correction because it is unflattering. It does not invent a reviewer, a test result or a credential.

Independence and advertising

Advertisers do not choose which tools are built, what a formula says, or which result is shown. Advertising is served separately from the editorial content. Affiliate and sponsored links are disclosed on the Disclaimer page and marked in the link markup.

The site is funded by advertising and, where relevant, affiliate commissions. It does not sell user data, require an account, or charge for a result.

What the site does not do

It does not provide financial, legal, tax, medical or engineering advice. It does not verify a reader's local code, lender fees, insurance policy or medical history. It does not claim to be a professional service. The limits are spelled out on the Disclaimer page and repeated where a tool could be misread.
