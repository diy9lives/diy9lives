Why not just ×7?
📊
Pets age fastest early in life, then slow down — a 1-year-old dog is already roughly equivalent to a 31-year-old human, not 7. The old "multiply by 7" rule badly underestimates young pets and overestimates old ones.
🐕
Larger dog breeds age faster in their senior years than small breeds, despite maturing at a similar rate early on — this is why giant breeds often have shorter lifespans.
🐈
Cats follow a more consistent curve: about 15 human years in the first year, 24 by age 2, then roughly 4 more human years per cat year after that.

How it works

For dogs, the tool uses a logarithmic formula: human age = 16 × ln(dog age) + 31, which rises quickly in the first years and then slows. A dog under one year scales up to 31 at age one. For dogs older than seven, a size adjustment adds extra human years per dog year, because larger breeds tend to age faster later in life: none for small dogs, about 1 more per year for medium, 2.5 for large, and 4 for giant. Cats are counted as 15 human years at age one, 24 at age two, and 4 more for each year after.

Frequently asked questions

Why not just multiply by 7?

Pets age fastest early in life and slower later. A one-year-old dog is closer to a young adult than a seven-year-old child, so the old rule badly misjudges both puppies and seniors.

Where does the dog formula come from?

It is based on a 2020 study that compared DNA changes in Labrador retrievers with those in humans. The size adjustment for older dogs is a rough estimate added by this tool.

Is this exact for my pet?

No. Breed, genetics, health, and lifestyle all affect aging. Treat the result as a fun and useful guide and ask your veterinarian about your pet's life stage.

What about mixed breeds?

Pick the size category that matches your dog's adult weight. That tends to matter more than the exact breed mix.

Using the number

A 5-year-old dog, any size, is 16 times the natural log of 5, plus 31, which is about 57 human years. The size adjustment does not start until age 7, so a 5-year-old Great Dane and a 5-year-old terrier get the same number here. A 10-year-old large dog does not: the log formula is about 68, and this page then adds extra years for the three years past 7. That add-on is the tool’s, not the paper’s.

A 10-year-old cat is 24 at age 2 plus 4 years for each of the next 8, which is 56. The FAQ already says the times-7 rule is wrong and that the dog curve comes from Labrador DNA methylation. The decision is whether your dog is a senior who needs a vet visit schedule, not whether the dog is “really” 57. Breed, weight, and teeth will tell your vet more than the logarithm.

Sources & references

The dog equation is from a 2020 paper on Labrador retrievers. The cat steps and the large-dog adjustment are not in that paper.

  • Wang et al., Cell Systems, 2020, PubMed 32619550. Reports human age = 16 ln(dog age) + 31 from Labrador methylomes.
  • The same paper on PubMed Central. Full text of the study, including the sentence that combines the two fits into 16 ln(dog age) + 31.
