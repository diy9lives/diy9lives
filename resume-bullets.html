How it works

Paste your duties in plain English, one per line, add your role and industry, and choose a tone. The tool rewrites each duty as a bullet that opens with a strong action verb and follows a results-first structure, nudging you to add numbers. It uses templates and word lists in your browser, so nothing you type leaves your device.

Frequently asked questions

What is an ATS?

An applicant tracking system scans resumes for keywords before a person reads them. Honestly mirroring the exact wording in the job posting helps your resume get through.

Why avoid starting with “Responsible for”?

It describes a duty, not a result. An action verb followed by an outcome shows what you actually accomplished.

How do I add numbers if I don't have exact data?

Use honest estimates and ranges, such as team size, customers served, time saved, or percent improved. Never invent numbers, and be ready to explain them in an interview.

How many bullets should each job have?

Three to six for recent roles and fewer for older ones. Lead with your strongest, most relevant achievements.

Using the number

“Responsible for invoices” wants to become “Issued invoices,” and it still has no number. The FAQ says to add a real count if you have one: “Issued 25 invoices a month and cut average payment time from 40 days to 28.” If you do not have the 28, do not type it because the bullet looks weak. A false metric is worse than a plain verb. The tool cannot check the claim.

Applicant-tracking systems mostly read text. The FAQ’s point about “responsible for” is that it hides the verb. Two bullets you can defend in an interview beat six that a template inflated.
