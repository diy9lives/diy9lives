How to use this calculator

Concrete is ordered by volume, so the only thing that matters is the shape you are filling and how deep it is.

  1. Measure the slab in feet, length and width. For an L-shaped patio, split it into rectangles and add them as separate pours.
  2. Set the thickness. Four inches is the usual walkway, patio, and shed slab. A driveway or anything a truck parks on is commonly five to six inches, and the thickness is what changes the order most.
  3. Pick a waste percentage. Ten percent is the page's default and a sensible one if the base is uneven. A form built on a screeded, compacted base can use less.
  4. Choose bags or yards. The tool gives both, so use the bag count to see whether this is a wheelbarrow job or a truck.
  5. For a round pad, switch modes and enter the diameter. The same cylinder math does a fence post hole, which is just a round pad standing on end.

A worked example: a 12 by 16 shed slab

At 6 inches thick, the volume is 12 × 16 × 6 ÷ 12, which is 96 cubic feet. Ten percent extra makes 105.6 cubic feet, and dividing by 27 gives 3.91 cubic yards.

In bags, that is 105.6 ÷ 0.60, or 176 bags of 80 pound mix, rounded up to 177. That is nearly four pallets. Lifting them, mixing them, and moving them into the form before the first batch starts to set is a full day with help, and the truck becomes the obvious answer at this size.

Now the deck posts. Six holes, 8 inches across and 3 feet deep: the radius is 4 inches, or 0.333 feet, so each hole is 3.14 × 0.333 × 0.333 × 3, about 1.05 cubic feet. Six holes is 6.28 cubic feet, or 0.23 cubic yards, which is 11 bags. The slab and the posts together come to 188 bags, and that combination is exactly how a project that looked like a bag job turns into a truck order plus a few bags on the side.

What your results mean

Cubic yards are what a ready-mix dispatcher wants to hear. Bags are what you can carry. The conversion is the whole argument: one cubic yard holds about 45 bags of 80 pound mix, so the two numbers describe the same pour in different units.

Bag counts are rounded up, and that rounding is not waste: a half bag of opened mix is a half bag you have to use or throw away. Buy the whole bag.

The order does not include the base or the reinforcement. A slab poured straight onto dirt cracks, and the compacted gravel base and any mesh or rebar are separate line items with their own quantities. The calculator also cannot tell you about slope, control joints, or drainage, all of which decide whether the slab is still flat in five years.

Ready-mix is sold in whole yards with a minimum order and often a short-load charge below it, so a 1.63 yard pour can price out worse than the bag count suggests. Ask the supplier for the minimum and the fee before you compare the two.

Frequently asked questions

How many 80 pound bags make a yard?

At 0.60 cubic feet per bag, 27 cubic feet is 45 bags. Sixty pound bags at 0.45 cubic feet need 60, and 50 pound bags at 0.375 need 72. Confirm the yield on the bag you are actually buying, because high-strength blends differ.

How thick should a patio slab be?

Four inches for foot traffic, grills, and patio furniture. Five to six inches for a driveway, a hot tub, or anything with a vehicle on it. Your local building department has the last word on footings and frost depth.

Do I need rebar or mesh?

Mesh helps hold cracks together; it does not stop them. Rebar matters more on structural slabs and driveways. Neither is included in this calculation, and neither fixes a bad base.

How long before I can walk on it?

Usually 24 to 48 hours for foot traffic, about a week before a car, and around 28 days for full design strength. Cold, wet weather slows all three, and the first day is when it needs to stay wet, not dry out.

Can I pour in cold weather?

Concrete needs to stay above freezing while it cures. Below roughly 40°F, fresh concrete should be protected, and frozen ground under a slab is a separate problem. Hot, windy days cause the opposite issue: the surface dries before it cures, so it needs covering.

Why is my bag count higher than the bag says?

Because of the waste percentage and the up-rounding. This page adds 10% for an uneven base and spills before dividing, and then rounds to a whole bag. The bag's own chart assumes a perfect rectangle with no loss.
