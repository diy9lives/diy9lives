How it works

The tip is your bill times the tip percentage. The total is the bill plus the tip, and the per-person amount is the total divided by the number of people splitting it. The quick tip guide shows common percentages so you can compare the cost at each level.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I tip?

In the United States, 15% to 20% is typical at restaurants for good service, and many people tip more for exceptional service. Customs differ in other countries.

Should I tip on the pre-tax amount?

Many people tip on the pre-tax subtotal, while others tip on the total. Enter whichever amount you prefer as the bill.

What if a service charge is already included?

Check your receipt. If a gratuity or service charge is already added, extra tipping is optional.

How do I split unevenly?

Enter each person's share as a separate bill, or calculate the shared items first and add personal items afterward.

Using the number

An $86.40 bill at 18% is a $15.55 tip and a $101.95 total. Split three ways, each person owes $33.98. At 20% the tip is $17.28 and each share is $34.56. The argument about 18 versus 20 is about 58 cents a person on this check.

If you tip on the pre-tax subtotal and tax was $6.40, the base is $80 and an 18% tip is $14.40, $1.15 less than tipping on the total. Either habit is coherent. Mixing them, or tipping again on a check that already has a service charge, is how tables overpay. The FAQ already flags the service-charge line. Read it before you move the slider.
