How to read the score
📈
90–100: Very easy, understood by an average 11-year-old. 60–70: Plain English, easily understood by 13–15 year olds. 0–30: Very difficult, best understood by university graduates.
💬
Most web and marketing copy targets 60–70 — conversational and widely accessible. Academic and legal writing often scores much lower, which isn't necessarily bad, just denser.
⚠
Syllable counting is approximate (based on vowel-group patterns), so scores are a close estimate, not a certified linguistic measurement.

How it works

The tool counts words, sentences, and syllables. The Flesch Reading Ease score is 206.835 − 1.015 × (words per sentence) − 84.6 × (syllables per word), limited to 0 through 100, where higher means easier. The Flesch-Kincaid grade level is 0.39 × (words per sentence) + 11.8 × (syllables per word) − 15.59. Syllables are estimated from vowel patterns, so the scores are close approximations.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good readability score?

For general audiences, 60 to 70 is considered plain English that most people understand. Technical, legal, and academic writing often scores lower, which is fine for the right readers.

How can I make my writing easier to read?

Use shorter sentences, simpler words, and active voice. Break long paragraphs into smaller ones.

Does a higher score always mean better writing?

No. It only measures sentence length and word length. Clear ideas, good structure, and the right tone matter just as much.

Why might the syllable count be off?

English spelling is irregular, so syllables are estimated from vowel groups. A few words will be miscounted, which slightly moves the score.

Using the number

“The cat sat on the mat.” is short words and one short sentence, so the Flesch score lands in the easy band. A 30-word sentence full of Latin abstracts will not, even if a specialist reader finds it clear. The FAQ already says a higher score is not always better writing. The decision use is narrower: if you are writing for the public and the score is under 50, look for the longest sentence and cut it. Then look for the syllable estimate, because “area” and “created” are where vowel counting goes wrong.

Plain-language guidance from the federal site is about the reader, not about hitting a Flesch number. Use the score to find the sentence you should reread. Do not pay a client for a particular grade.
