Pro painting tips
🎨
Always buy 10% extra. Batch variations between cans are real, and you will need touch-up paint later. Better to return a can than run out mid-wall.
✏
Primer is not optional on new drywall or when going from dark to light. Skip it and you will need 3+ coats. With primer, 2 coats is usually enough.
🌐
Flat vs eggshell vs satin: Flat hides imperfections (ceilings, low-traffic). Eggshell is best for most walls. Satin or semi-gloss for bathrooms, kitchens, and trim.

How it works

For each room, wall area is 2 × (length + width) × height. The tool subtracts about 20 square feet per door and 15 per window to get the paintable area. Gallons needed are paintable area × number of coats ÷ coverage per gallon, summed across rooms, then increased by 10% for waste and rounded up to whole gallons. Cost is gallons times the price tier you choose. The calculation covers walls only, not ceilings or trim.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include ceilings and trim?

No. It calculates wall paint only. Measure ceilings (length × width) and trim separately and add them if you are painting them.

How much does a gallon of paint cover?

Most paint covers roughly 350 to 450 square feet per gallon per coat, depending on the product and how smooth or textured the surface is. The label on your can has the manufacturer's number.

Do I really need primer?

Primer helps on new drywall, bare patches, stains, and big color changes such as dark to light. It usually saves you from adding extra coats of expensive paint.

Why add 10% extra?

Batches can vary slightly in color, you will lose some paint to the roller and tray, and leftovers are handy for touch-ups later.

Using the number

A 12 by 10 foot room with 9 foot walls, one door, and two windows has 396 square feet of wall and 346 after the door and windows. Two coats at 400 square feet per gallon is 1.73 gallons. The 10% buffer makes it 1.90, and the page rounds up to 2 gallons. Paint is sold by the gallon, so the extra fraction is not optional.

The ceiling is 120 square feet. Two coats at the same coverage is 0.60 gallons, about 0.66 with the buffer. Walls plus ceiling cross 2 gallons and land on 3. That is the decision the FAQ points at without doing the sum: measure the ceiling if you are painting it, because it can be the gallon that changes the budget. The 20 and 15 square foot allowances are this page’s averages for a standard door and a standard window. A sliding door or a bay window is bigger. Read the coverage on the can. The 350 / 400 / 450 presets are a range, not a brand’s promise.

If the house was built before 1978, sanding and scraping paint is a lead question, not just a quantity question. EPA’s renovation rule exists because of that.
