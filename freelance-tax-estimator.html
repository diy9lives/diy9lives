How it works

Net profit is your income minus business expenses. Self-employment tax is 15.3% of 92.35% of that profit: 12.4% for Social Security, applied only up to the $184,500 wage base (reduced by any W-2 wages you also earn), plus 2.9% for Medicare. A 0.9% Medicare surtax applies above $200,000 for single and head-of-household filers and $250,000 for joint filers.

Half of the self-employment tax is deductible. The tool then subtracts the 2026 standard deduction and, if you leave it checked, the 20% qualified business income (QBI) deduction when your taxable income is below $201,750 (single or head of household) or $403,500 (joint). Brackets come from IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32. The federal figure shown is only the extra income tax your freelance income creates, compared with your other income alone.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I set aside from each payment?

The “of net profit” percentage shows what share of your freelance profit this estimate sets aside. Many freelancers move that percentage into a separate savings account every time they get paid.

When are quarterly estimated taxes due?

Estimated payments are generally due in mid-April, mid-June, mid-September, and mid-January. If you are reading this late in the year, the final payment for the year is typically due in January.

What is the QBI deduction?

It lets many self-employed people deduct up to 20% of their qualified business income. This tool applies it only below the income thresholds, because above them extra limits apply that need a tax professional.

Does this include state income tax?

No. This is federal only. If your state taxes income, add your state's rate on top of the amount shown.

Can I avoid underpayment penalties?

The IRS has safe-harbor rules based on paying a share of this year's tax or of last year's tax through the year. Ask a tax professional which applies to you, since it depends on your income and prior-year return.

Using the number

Take $80,000 of 1099 receipts and $12,000 of ordinary expenses, single, no other W-2 wages, with the QBI box on. Profit is $68,000. Self-employment tax is about $9,608. The federal income tax after the standard deduction and the simplified QBI deduction is about $4,273. Together that is about $13,881 for the year, or about $3,470 a quarter.

A practical rule while you are waiting on a preparer: move 25% of each deposit into a separate account if your expenses are light, and rerun this page once a month with year-to-date profit. On this example, $13,881 is about 20% of the $68,000 profit and about 17% of the $80,000 that hit the bank. Setting aside 25% of deposits would overshoot a bit, which is safer than coming up short in April. If you also have a W-2 job, put those wages in the other-earned-income box. They use up Social Security room and change the income-tax brackets.

This estimate skips state income tax. In Pennsylvania that is another 3.07% of profit, and many towns add a local earned income tax on net profits. It also skips the QBI limits that appear once taxable income is over the threshold printed on the page, and it will not raise the Social Security wage base to the $192,300 Social Security lists for 2027.
