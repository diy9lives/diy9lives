Quick form

The fastest route for a bug report, a tool suggestion, or joining the newsletter list is the short form: open the form. It asks the same questions as the checklist below.

What to expect

This is a small site and replies can take a few days. Everything sent to that address is read by the person who maintains the tools, not by a support queue, which is why the reply rate is not instant.

A bug that changes an answer gets priority over a cosmetic issue, and a wrong figure with a source attached gets fixed fastest of all. If you report something and do not hear back, send it again; an email lost to a spam filter is not a decision.

Reporting a bug

These four details turn a report into a fix:

  • Which tool — the page name, for example the paint calculator.
  • What you entered — the actual numbers in each field, not a description of them.
  • What you expected, and what you saw — both, because either one alone is ambiguous.
  • Your device and browser — iPhone with Safari, or Windows with Chrome, for example.

A screenshot helps when a layout breaks. For a wrong number, the numbers you typed matter more than the picture, since the arithmetic can be reproduced without seeing your screen.

Reporting a figure that looks wrong

Most tools here depend on an outside figure: a tax bracket, a wage base, a bag yield, a sizing chart, a coverage rate. If one is out of date or simply incorrect, the most useful message includes:

  • The page and the specific figure.
  • The source you are comparing it against, with a link if you have one.
  • The year that source applies to.

Pages show when they were last updated, and the date changes when the content does. A figure that was right for one tax year and is being read in the next one is exactly the kind of error worth reporting.

Suggesting a tool

Requests are welcome, and several tools on the site started as one. The suggestions that get built first tend to be ones where the arithmetic is clear, the inputs are things a person actually knows about themselves, and the answer changes a decision rather than just being interesting.

It also helps to say what you would do with the result. A tool that produces a number nobody acts on is a calculator without a purpose.

About the numbers themselves

These tools are planning estimates. They are not tax, legal, financial, or medical advice, and they cannot see your actual situation. If a result conflicts with your lender, your accountant, or your clinician, they are right and the page is the approximation. The about page describes where the figures come from and how corrections are handled, and the terms set out the limits.

Privacy

Nothing you type into a calculator is sent anywhere or stored; the arithmetic runs in your browser. The one thing to be aware of is that the VIN decoder and the currency converter do call public services to do their job. The privacy policy has the details.

Email sent to the address above arrives in a normal mailbox. Ask before sending anything sensitive, because a bug report rarely needs personal financial details, and a made-up set of numbers that reproduces the problem is just as useful.
