Quick answers

What counts as an asset vs. a liability?

An asset is anything you own with monetary value (cash, investments, property, vehicles). A liability is anything you owe (loans, credit card balances, mortgages). Net worth is simply assets minus liabilities — it can be negative, especially early in life or right after taking on student loans, and that's normal.

Should I include my car and home at full value?

Use realistic current market value, not what you originally paid or the outstanding loan balance. A car depreciates over time, so check a valuation tool like Kelley Blue Book for a current estimate. For your home, a recent appraisal or a conservative estimate from a real estate site works better than the original purchase price.

How often should I recalculate this?

Most people find checking net worth quarterly or annually useful for tracking progress without getting anxious over short-term market swings. Since nothing here is saved, you'll need to note your result somewhere (a spreadsheet, notes app) if you want to track it over time.

How it works

The calculator adds up every asset field to get total assets, adds up every liability field to get total liabilities, and subtracts liabilities from assets. It runs entirely in your browser, and nothing is saved or sent anywhere, so closing the page clears it.

Using the number

A house worth $350,000 with a $240,000 mortgage is $110,000 of net worth, not $350,000. The car belongs in assets at what you could sell it for this month, and the loan belongs in liabilities. Counting the car at the price you paid and also ignoring the loan is how a worksheet flatters you. Retirement accounts count. The sofa usually does not, unless you would actually sell it.

A negative result means the loans are larger than the things you could sell. That is common the year you buy a house or finish a degree, and it is not a moral score. The useful comparison is the same worksheet six months from now. If liabilities are almost all credit cards, the debt payoff planner is the next page. If they are almost all a mortgage at a low rate, paying extra principal is a different decision from paying a 22% card, and this single number will not tell them apart.

Nothing is saved. If you want the figure later, print the page or write the total down. The calculator will not remember it.
