Quick percentage formulas
💡
To find 10%: just move the decimal point one place left. 10% of 350 = 35. For 5%, halve that. For 20%, double it. Mental math hack.
📈
Percentage change formula: (New - Old) / Old x 100. If something went from $80 to $100, that is a 25% increase, not 20%.
🎭
Tip shortcut: For an 18% tip, find 10% (move decimal), add half of that (5%), then add another 3%. Total = 10+5+3 = 18%.

How it works

The page solves four common problems. “What is X% of Y” is X ÷ 100 × Y. “X is what % of Y” is X ÷ Y × 100. Percentage change is (new − old) ÷ old × 100. Add or remove a percentage multiplies the number by 1 plus or minus the percentage ÷ 100. Each result updates as you type.

Frequently asked questions

Why is going from 80 to 100 a 25% increase and not 20%?

Percentage change is measured against the starting value. The increase is 20, and 20 ÷ 80 is 25%.

How can I find 15% in my head?

Find 10% by moving the decimal one place left, then add half of that for another 5%. For $60, 10% is $6, 5% is $3, so 15% is $9.

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

Going from 10% to 12% is a rise of 2 percentage points, but a 20% increase in the rate itself.

Does a 20% discount followed by a 20% increase get me back to the start?

No. $100 down 20% is $80, and $80 up 20% is $96, because the second change is based on a smaller number.

Using the number

The FAQ says a 20% discount and then a 20% increase does not get you home. In dollars: $100 down 20% is $80, and $80 up 20% is $96. You are short $4 because the second 20% was taken on a smaller base. The same trap shows up in a sale. A $64 item at 15% off is $9.60 off, $54.40. Finding 15% in your head, which the FAQ suggests, is 10% ($6.40) plus half of that ($3.20).

Percent change from 80 to 100 is 25%, not 20%, because the base is 80. Going from a 10% fee to a 12% fee is 2 percentage points and also a 20% increase in the fee.
