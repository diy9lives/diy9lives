How it works

Start with the monthly take-home pay you want, add business expenses and a savings buffer, then gross it up for taxes: (take-home + expenses + buffer) ÷ (1 − tax buffer %). That is the revenue you need each month.

Next, your billable hours: weekly hours × working weeks (52 minus vacation weeks) ÷ 12, reduced by your non-billable overhead percentage for admin, marketing, and unpaid time. Dividing the revenue you need by those hours gives your minimum hourly rate. The recommended rate adds 25% on top for negotiating room.

Frequently asked questions

What tax buffer should I use?

Freelancers pay self-employment tax plus income tax, and a common rule of thumb is to set aside roughly 25% to 30%. It depends on your income and location. Our 1099 Tax Estimator gives a closer number.

What counts as non-billable overhead?

Anything you do that you can't charge for directly: finding clients, sending proposals and invoices, bookkeeping, learning new skills, and unpaid revisions. Many freelancers find 20% to 40% is realistic.

Why is the recommended rate higher than the minimum?

The minimum only breaks even. The recommended rate adds 25% of cushion so you can negotiate, absorb slow months, and still hit your goals.

Should I charge hourly or by the project?

Either can work. Hourly is simple and protects you on open-ended work, while project pricing can earn more when you are efficient. Your hourly number is still useful for pricing a project.
