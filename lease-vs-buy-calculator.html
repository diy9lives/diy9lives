How this is calculated
💰
Cost to lease = money due at signing + all monthly payments over the term. You never build equity, and you hand the car back at the end.
📈
Cost to buy = down payment + loan payments made during your hold period, minus the equity you've built (resale value minus remaining loan balance) when you sell or trade in.
⚖
Leasing usually wins for shorter terms (2–3 years) since you avoid the steepest depreciation years. Buying usually wins if you keep the car 5+ years, since the loan gets paid off and the car still has value.

Worked example: $38,000 SUV, 36 months

A $35,000 car, kept three years. Lease at $450 a month with $2,000 at signing. Buy with $3,500 down at 6.5% over 60 months:

Nearly a wash: $618 apart over three years, or about $17 a month. The lease is marginally cheaper in cash, and it carries no resale risk — if the market drops, the lessor absorbs it. Buying leaves you with a car worth $20,706 and a loan of $13,836, and turning that equity into money means selling.

The cost you do not see in the payment

The comparison is only fair when both sides cover the same period. A three-year lease against a five-year loan compares a cost with a cost plus an asset, which is why the page subtracts the equity from the purchase side. Change the years and the answer often flips: over five years the lease is a second lease, while the buyer has a paid-off car.

Mileage limits are the hidden cost of leasing. A 12,000-mile-a-year allowance sounds generous until a long commute uses it up, and the excess charge per mile at the end can wipe out the entire advantage. Wear charges work the same way. If your usage is unpredictable, a lease transfers that risk to you rather than away from you.

Buying ties up capital and exposes you to depreciation, which is largest in the first three years — exactly the period a lease covers. That is the trade in one sentence: a lease pays for the worst years of depreciation without asking you to own them. Whether that is worth $17 a month depends on how much you value the option to walk away.

Lease or buy questions

Why is the lease sometimes cheaper?

Because you are only paying for the depreciation during the term, not the whole car. In a period when resale values are strong, that can work out close to the cost of owning.

What counts in the amount due at signing?

Everything you pay before driving away: the cap cost reduction, the first payment, fees and tax. The calculator takes the total.

Should I put money down on a lease?

Usually no. If the car is written off, that money is gone, and it only lowers the payment rather than the total cost. Keep it in your account.

Can I end a lease early?

Usually at a cost. Early termination charges vary by contract and can be steep, so treat the term as fixed.

What about buying out the lease at the end?

It is often possible at a set residual price. Compare that price to the market value then; if the car is worth more, buying out is a good deal, and if less, it is not.
