Why stacked discounts don't just add
🧠
Each discount applies to the price after the previous one, not the original price. $100 at 20% off is $80. An extra 10% off that $80 is $72 — a 28% total discount, not 30%.
💵
Tax is calculated on the final discounted price in most states, applied after all discounts.

How it works

Each discount is applied one after another to the price left by the previous one, so two discounts never simply add. The final price multiplies the original by (1 − each discount), in order. Tax, if you enter it, is calculated on the discounted price. The savings are the original price minus the discounted price, and the effective discount is that savings as a percent of the original.

Frequently asked questions

Why isn't 20% off plus an extra 10% off equal to 30% off?

The second discount applies to the already-reduced price. $100 at 20% off is $80, and 10% off that is $72, a 28% total discount.

Does tax apply before or after the discount?

In most places sales tax is charged on the discounted price. Enter your local rate to see the final total.

Does the order of discounts matter?

For percentage discounts, no. The final price is the same in any order, though a fixed-dollar coupon applied first or last can change the result.

Can I use this to compare deals?

Yes. The effective discount makes it easy to compare offers like “40% off” against “30% off plus 15% off.”
