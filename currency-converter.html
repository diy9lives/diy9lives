Quick answers

Why does my bank give me a different rate?

This tool shows the mid-market rate — the real, unmarked exchange rate used between banks. Consumer services (your bank, PayPal, airport kiosks) add a markup on top, often 1–4% for banks and cards, and sometimes more at airport currency exchanges. Wise and a few other services are known for charging closer to the mid-market rate with a transparent fee.

How often are rates updated?

Once per business day, around 4pm CET, based on European Central Bank reference rates. Rates don't update on weekends or ECB holidays, so if you check on a Saturday you'll see Friday's rate. This is standard for free reference-rate APIs; real-time forex trading rates fluctuate by the second and aren't publicly available for free.

Can I convert cryptocurrency here?

No, this tool covers standard fiat (government-issued) world currencies only. Crypto exchange rates move far more rapidly and would need a different data source.

How it works

Your browser requests the latest rates from the Frankfurter API, which publishes reference exchange rates from institutions such as the European Central Bank, updated each working day. The tool multiplies your amount by the rate for the pair you choose. Rates are reference rates, not live trading quotes, and do not include the markups banks, card networks, and exchange counters add.

Using the number

If the reference rate is 0.92 euro per dollar, $1,000 is €920 on this page. A bank or card that takes 2% off the rate gives you about €901.60. The €18 is the markup, and it is invisible if you only remember “about 0.92.” Weekend visits show Friday’s rate, which the page already says, because the ECB does not publish a Saturday fix.

Do not use the figure to settle a contract that specifies a Bloomberg or Federal Reserve rate on a named day. This is a mid-market reference for planning a trip or checking an invoice. For the invoice itself, type the rate you actually agreed, not yesterday’s ECB print.
