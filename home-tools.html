Measure twice,
buy once.

The expensive part of a home project is usually the second trip to the store. These calculators turn the dimensions you already have into the quantity you actually need, with the waste and depth assumptions stated.

Every quantity here is area or volume times a coverage rate. The rate is where projects go wrong: paint coverage depends on the surface, concrete bag yield depends on the mix, mulch settles, and flooring waste depends on the room's shape. Each page names its rate and lets you change it.

Measure in feet and inches, write the numbers down, and buy the next whole box or bag. The calculator's job is to make the last row and the last bag predictable.

What the home tools cannot see

  • The condition of the surface. Porous drywall, bare wood and exterior siding all change paint coverage.
  • Slope, irregular shapes and sub-base compaction. Order extra for anything that is not a flat rectangle.
  • Local code and structural requirements. A slab or footing may need an engineer, not a calculator.
  • Room heat gain from windows, insulation and sun. The BTU tool uses a sizing shortcut, not a Manual J load calculation.
