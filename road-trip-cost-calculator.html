How it works

Total miles are your one-way distance, doubled if you check round trip. Gallons are miles ÷ MPG, and fuel cost is gallons × the gas price. Lodging is hotel nights × the nightly rate. Fuel, tolls and parking, lodging, and food and extras are added into a trip total, which is divided by the number of travelers to get what each person owes.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use highway MPG or the combined rating?

Use a figure that reflects your trip. Highway driving at high speeds, a full car, and air conditioning all lower real MPG, so subtracting a few MPG makes a safer budget.

How do I split costs fairly?

The per-person number divides everything evenly, including the driver's share. If someone joins for only part of the trip or skips the hotel, adjust the traveler count or work out those costs separately.

What should I include in the food and extras box?

Meals, snacks, activities, parking, and a small cushion for surprises. Many travelers add 10% to 15% for unexpected costs.

Does this include wear and tear on my car?

No. If you want to count it, add the IRS-style per-mile cost of operating a car or an amount of your own into the extras box.

Using the number

A 900-mile round trip in a 30 mpg car at $3.50 a gallon is 30 gallons and $105 of fuel. Two nights at $140 is $280. Tolls and a couple of meals can pass the fuel bill without trying. If the calculator says fuel is the headline, check the hotel line before you optimize tire pressure.

Dividing the total by the number of people is fair when everyone rides and everyone would have paid for a room. It is unfair when one person drove their own car and will eat the maintenance, or when one person has a hotel point balance and the other does not. The wear on the car is not in the total. A rough private add is the IRS mileage idea or your own tires-and-oil cost, but do not present it as a precise share unless the group agreed beforehand.
