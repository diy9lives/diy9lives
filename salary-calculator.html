How it works

Your combined gross income from all jobs is reduced by your 401(k) percentage, which is pre-tax for federal income tax. Federal taxable income is that amount minus the 2026 standard deduction for your filing status, taxed through the 2026 progressive brackets. Social Security is 6.2% up to the $184,500 wage base, and Medicare is 1.45% with an extra 0.9% on wages over $200,000 ($250,000 married filing jointly). State and local tax are flat percentages you can edit, and health insurance is subtracted at the end. The result is shown per year, per month, and per hour.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my real paycheck different from this estimate?

Employers withhold based on your W-4 and use their own pay periods and benefit deductions. Credits, other pre-tax benefits, and your state's real rules also change the result, so this is an estimate.

How accurate is the state tax?

It applies one flat rate to your income. Most states actually use brackets, deductions, or credits, so override the rate if you know your effective state rate.

What is the standard deduction?

It is a fixed amount of income you do not pay federal income tax on if you do not itemize. For 2026 it is $16,100 for single filers, $32,200 for married filing jointly, and $24,150 for heads of household.

Does this include tax credits?

No. Credits such as the child tax credit would reduce your tax further and are not modeled here.
