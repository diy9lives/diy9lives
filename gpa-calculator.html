Quick answers

What grade scale does this use?

Standard US 4.0 scale: A/A+ = 4.0, A- = 3.7, B+ = 3.3, B = 3.0, B- = 2.7, C+ = 2.3, C = 2.0, C- = 1.7, D+ = 1.3, D = 1.0, F = 0.0. Some schools use slightly different scales (a few give A+ a 4.3) — check your school's official policy if you need an exact match.

How is weighted GPA calculated?

Each course's grade points are multiplied by its credit hours to get "quality points." All quality points are summed and divided by total credit hours. A 4-credit A (4.0) counts more than a 1-credit A, which is why higher-credit courses affect your GPA more.

How do I combine this with my existing GPA?

Enter your current cumulative GPA and total credit hours earned so far in the card above. The calculator treats your prior GPA as if it were one large course worth that many credits, then blends it with the new courses to give your updated overall GPA.

How it works

Each course's grade is converted to grade points on the standard 4.0 scale, then multiplied by its credit hours to get quality points. Your GPA is total quality points divided by total credit hours, so a 4-credit course counts more than a 1-credit one. If you enter a prior GPA and credits, the tool blends them by weighting each by its credit hours to give a cumulative GPA.

Using the number

Three courses: an A in 3 credits (4.0 × 3 = 12), a B+ in 4 credits (3.3 × 4 = 13.2), and an A− in 3 credits (3.7 × 3 = 11.1). That is 36.3 points over 10 credits, a term GPA of 3.63. Fold in a prior 3.20 over 30 credits, which is 96 points. Combined points are 132.3 over 40 credits, a cumulative GPA of 3.31.

Those grade points are a common plus/minus scale. They are not every college’s scale. Some schools give a B+ a 3.3, some a 3.33, and some do not use plus and minus at all. If your registrar publishes a different table, this number will not match the transcript. A pass/fail course usually adds credits without points, or adds neither. This page has no pass/fail button, so leave those courses off.
