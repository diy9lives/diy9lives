Kitchen math,
without the guesswork.

Scaling, converting and costing are three different problems. These tools keep them separate so a doubled recipe still tastes like the original and the price per serving is real.

A recipe is a ratio, a pan is an area, and a grocery receipt is a unit-price problem. The tools here handle each one directly. The scaler keeps fractions honest; the converter uses ingredient-specific gram weights where a cup is not a cup; the cost tool separates the pinch you use from the bag you buy.

For baking, weigh ingredients when you can. A cup of flour varies enough between scoops to change the result, which is why the converter gives a range and the scaler warns about leavening.

What the kitchen tools cannot see

  • Your flour, your scoop and your oven. Gram weights are reference values, not a guarantee.
  • Baking time and pan material. Scaling a batter changes depth and time; the guide explains the adjustment.
  • What you already have in the pantry. The cost tool counts what you buy, not what you use.
  • Dietary needs. These are quantity tools, not nutrition advice.
