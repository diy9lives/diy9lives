How it works

Over the years you plan to own the car, the calculator adds five costs. Depreciation comes from our standard curve (20%, 15%, 13%, 12%, 10%, 9%, then 8% a year). Loan interest is what you pay beyond the amount borrowed during those years. Insurance, maintenance, and fuel are your yearly estimates multiplied by the years owned. The total is divided by years and months to give a cost per year and per month, and the bar shows each cost's share.

Frequently asked questions

Why include depreciation?

It is usually the largest cost of owning a car, and it never shows up on a bank statement. A cheaper car that depreciates quickly can cost more per year than a pricier one that holds its value.

How do I estimate insurance for a car I don't own yet?

Get actual quotes for the specific models you are considering. Rates vary a lot by car, location, driving record, and age.

What maintenance cost should I use?

New cars usually cost less in the first few years because of warranties, while older cars cost more as parts wear. Use your own repair history if you have one, or ask owners of the same model.

Does this work for electric cars?

Yes. Use your yearly electricity cost in the fuel box, and consider typical differences in maintenance and insurance for EVs.

Using the number

Take the $35,000 new car from the depreciation page and keep it three years. The curve says it is worth about $20,700, so depreciation is about $14,300, or roughly $4,800 a year. A loan might add a couple of thousand dollars of interest. Insurance at $1,500 a year is $4,500. Fuel at $150 a month is $5,400. Maintenance might be small while the warranty lasts, or it might not. Add those and divide by three. The payment you were quoted is one slice.

The bar on the page is the decision tool. If depreciation is half the bar, a cheaper purchase price on a car that falls just as fast does not help, and a used car that has already taken the first two years can be the cheaper one to own. Get insurance quotes on the actual VIN. The number you invent here is the one most likely to be wrong, and it is large enough to change the winner.
