Sleep science facts
⏰
90-minute sleep cycles. Each cycle goes through light sleep, deep sleep, and REM. Waking mid-cycle causes sleep inertia (that groggy, disoriented feeling).
💢
Most adults need 5-6 full cycles (7.5-9 hours). Getting 6 cycles in is rare but powerful. Even 4 cycles is better than waking mid-cycle after 8 hours.
☀
Consistency beats duration. Going to sleep and waking at the same time every day (including weekends) is the single most impactful sleep habit.

How it works

Sleep moves through cycles that average about 90 minutes. If you choose a wake-up time, the tool counts backward from it by whole cycles (six down to one) plus the time you need to fall asleep, which defaults to 14 minutes and can be changed. If you choose a bedtime, it counts forward by the same amounts to show good wake-up times. Each option also shows the total sleep it gives you.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 90-minute cycle exact?

No. It is an average. Cycle length varies between people and from night to night, so treat the times as a helpful target rather than a precise schedule.

How much sleep do adults need?

Most adults do best with at least seven hours a night, and many need up to nine. Needs vary with age, health, and activity.

Why do I feel groggy even after a full night?

Waking in the middle of deeper sleep can cause grogginess called sleep inertia. A consistent schedule, a dark and cool room, and limiting late caffeine and screens also help.

What should I do if I can never fall asleep quickly?

Adjust the fall-asleep slider to match your real time. If trouble sleeping is frequent or affects your days, talk to a healthcare provider.

Using the number

Wake at 6:30 with the default 14 minutes to fall asleep. Six cycles is 9 hours of sleep plus 14 minutes, so you would need to be in bed at 9:16 p.m. Five cycles is 7 hours 30 minutes plus 14 minutes, in bed at 10:46 p.m. Four cycles is 6 hours 14 minutes of sleep, in bed at 12:16 a.m., which is a short night even if you wake between cycles.

The FAQ already says 90 minutes is an average and that adults generally need at least seven hours. Five cycles clears that. Four does not. If you regularly need 40 minutes to fall asleep, change the 14. Leaving it there will hand you a bedtime that is half an hour too late, and you will blame the cycles. Shift work and sleep disorders are outside the chart. NHLBI’s sleep pages are the place for that, not a cycle counter.
