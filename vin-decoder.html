How it works

A VIN is 17 characters and never contains the letters I, O, or Q, which are left out to avoid confusion with 1 and 0. The tool checks that your entry fits that pattern, then asks the U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration's public vPIC database to decode it. It shows the model year, make, model, trim, body style, engine, fuel type, drive type, transmission, plant country, vehicle type, and weight class when the manufacturer reported them.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I find my VIN?

Look at the base of the windshield on the driver's side, the sticker inside the driver's door jamb, or your registration and insurance documents.

Why are some fields blank?

Manufacturers report different amounts of detail, and older or low-volume vehicles often have fewer fields. A blank field means the database has nothing for it, not that the car lacks the feature.

Does this show accident or title history?

No. A VIN decoder only shows factory specifications. For accident, title, and odometer history, use a vehicle history report from a provider approved under the federal NMVTIS program or a service like Carfax.

Is my VIN stored?

The VIN you enter is sent to the NHTSA database to look it up. We do not store it.

Does it work for cars built outside the US?

The database is built around vehicles for the US market, so imports and some foreign-market vehicles may return limited information.

Using the number

A decode that comes back with the right model year and engine is a check that the car in the ad is the car on the title. It is not a salvage check, a mileage check, or a recall completion check. vPIC often leaves trim, color, and options blank, because those were not in the manufacturer’s submission. A blank is not a warning by itself. A year or a body style that disagrees with the listing is.

The ninth character is a check digit. If you mistype one character, vPIC may return nothing useful or a different vehicle. Read the VIN from the dash at the base of the windshield or the driver’s door, not from memory. The request leaves your browser. The privacy page says so. Do not paste a VIN into a random site if you are trying to avoid a seller. This one is the federal database.
