How it works

The principal-and-interest payment comes from the standard amortization formula, using your loan amount (home price minus down payment), interest rate, and term. Property tax and homeowners insurance, entered as yearly amounts, are divided by 12 and added, so the headline number is your full monthly housing payment before any HOA dues or mortgage insurance.

The table shows the first 12 months and splits each payment into principal and interest. Early payments are mostly interest, which is why extra principal payments made early save so much.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include PMI?

No. Private mortgage insurance usually applies when you put less than 20% down on a conventional loan. Ask your lender for the monthly estimate and add it to the total.

What interest rate should I enter?

Use the rate lenders actually quote for your credit profile and loan type. Rates change daily, so compare several quotes before relying on a number.

Why is so much of my early payment interest?

Interest is charged on the remaining balance, which is highest at the start. As the balance falls, the interest share shrinks and more of each payment goes to principal.

How much does a 15-year loan save compared with 30 years?

The monthly payment is higher, but you pay far less interest because you borrow for half as long. Run both terms to see the difference for your numbers.

How accurate is the total cost?

It adds principal, interest, and the tax and insurance you entered across the whole term. Taxes and premiums usually change over time, so treat it as a planning figure.
