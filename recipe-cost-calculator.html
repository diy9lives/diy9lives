Comparing cost-per-serving across recipes is one of the fastest ways to spot which meals are actually saving you money versus which just feel cheap.

Ingredients, package price and yield

Enter how many servings the recipe makes, then add each ingredient with what it cost you. The page totals the ingredients and divides by the serving count to give a cost per plate. Add rows only for what you actually used: half a bag of flour is the price of half a bag, not the price of the bag.

The serving count is the number that decides everything. A tray of lasagne "serves 8" in a cookbook and 5 in a hungry house, and the cost per serving moves by 60% between those two answers. Use the number of portions you really get.

Worked example: a pot of chili for six

A pot of chilli that makes six servings:

IngredientCost
Ground beef, 2 lb$11.98
Beans and tomatoes, 4 cans$4.76
Onion, peppers, garlic$3.10
Spices and stock$1.85
Total$21.69

At six servings that is $3.62 a portion. At five it is $4.34, and at eight, $2.71. The recipe did not change and neither did the shopping; only the divisor moved. That is why cost-per-serving comparisons between two recipes are only meaningful when both serving counts mean the same thing — weigh the finished dish or count the containers if you want a fair comparison.

Cost per serving, not per pot

The total is food cost, not the cost of the meal. It excludes the energy used to cook it, the foil and containers, and your time. For a household budget that is close enough. For pricing something you sell, it is not: a caterer or a small food business needs the full plate cost, which is why the usual rule of thumb multiplies ingredient cost by three to cover labour, overhead and waste. That multiplier is a starting convention, not a measured fact, and it varies by the kind of food you make.

Ingredient cost per serving is also where portion creep hides. A recipe costed at $3.62 assumes six equal bowls; if the actual bowl is a third larger, the real cost is $4.83 and the extra money is invisible in a grocery total.

The comparison worth doing is between meals you actually cook. Track four or five for a month and the pattern is clear: the expensive ones are usually expensive because of meat and cheese, and the cheap ones are cheap because of beans, rice and vegetables. That is not a moral judgement, just where the money is.

Recipe cost questions

Should I include pantry staples?

Include what you used, priced at what it cost you. Ignoring oil and spices makes every recipe look cheaper than it is, though the effect per serving is small.

How do I cost half a package?

Enter half the price. If a $5 bag of rice makes ten batches and you used one, the cost is $0.50.

Does this include electricity or gas?

No. An hour of oven use costs something, but the amount depends on your appliance and your tariff, so add it yourself if you are pricing food to sell.

Why is my cost per serving higher than a restaurant's?

Restaurants buy in volume, portion precisely, and count every gram. A home recipe costed honestly usually is more expensive per plate than a cheap takeaway.

Can I use this to price something I sell?

Use it for the ingredient line only. You still need packaging, labour, overhead, and whatever your local rules require for selling food.
