Car math,
from the sidewall out.

The tire size calculator is the flagship. The rest of this cluster answers the questions around it: what the car really costs, what the deal really says, and what changes when the wheels do.

A car is a stack of numbers that rarely appear together: the note, the insurance, the fuel, the tires, the depreciation, the repair that arrives in month fourteen. These tools put the stack in one place and show which line moves the answer.

The tire calculator matches your stock size against sizes that are actually stocked, then shows the speedometer error each one causes. It is the page David has put the most work into, and it is the best place to start if you are changing wheels.

Read the deal, then the math

The car guides are written around the sequence of an actual purchase: the amount you will write a check for, the payment for two terms, the depreciation you cannot see, and the tire change that comes later.

How to read a car dealTire size and speedometer error

What the car tools cannot see

  • Your insurance quote, credit tier and the dealer's actual fees. Those come from the lender and the paperwork.
  • Fitment clearance. The tire tool matches diameter; offset, width and suspension still decide whether it rubs.
  • Load index and speed rating. A size that fits can still be the wrong tire for the vehicle.
  • Condition. A VIN tells you what the car was built as, not whether it was maintained.
