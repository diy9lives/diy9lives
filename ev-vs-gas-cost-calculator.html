Setting up both cars honestly

Enter your yearly miles, the local gas price, and the MPG of the gas car you are comparing against. Then put in your home electricity rate and the EV's efficiency in kilowatt-hours per 100 miles, which is how window stickers state it. If you charge away from home, set the share and the public charging price.

The price premium field is what turns fuel savings into a decision. Put in the difference between the two cars' purchase prices, not the full price of the EV.

Worked example: 12,000 miles a year

12,000 miles a year, gas at $3.50, a 30 MPG car, electricity at 17 cents a kWh, an EV using 30 kWh per 100 miles, all charging at home, and a $5,000 premium:

Gas car (400 gal)$1,400

Break-even on the $5,000 premium6.3 years

Cost per mile tells the same story more sharply: 11.7 cents for the gas car against 5.1 cents for the EV. Now move half the charging to a public fast charger at 45 cents a kWh and the EV's yearly cost rises to about $978, the saving falls to $422, and the payback stretches past eleven years.

When the EV breaks even

The fuel comparison is arithmetic and the purchase decision is not. A six-year payback is fine if you keep cars for ten years and poor if you trade every four. That is why the premium field exists: the fuel saving is a fact about miles and prices, and the payback is a fact about how long you hold the car.

Home charging is the whole economics. Electricity at a residential rate is cheap per mile; public fast charging is often within a few cents of petrol. Anyone who cannot charge at home should model a high public share before assuming an EV is cheaper to run, and the calculator will show the saving shrinking as that share rises.

Two costs are missing and both are real. Insurance for some EVs runs higher because repairs cost more. And maintenance runs lower, since there is no oil, no exhaust and less brake wear from regenerative braking. Those roughly offset, but neither is small enough to ignore when you are deciding.

EV cost questions

What is a realistic kWh per 100 miles?

Around 25 to 35 for most efficient cars, higher in cold weather and at sustained highway speed. Use the figure from the car's own sticker as a start.

Does the calculator include home charger installation?

No. Add it to the premium if you need one installed, since it is a real cost of choosing the EV.

Why does public charging hurt the maths so much?

Because it is often two to three times the residential rate. A third of your miles on fast charging can halve the saving.

Are EVs cheaper to maintain?

Usually less in routine service and more in Tyres and body repair. It depends on the car, so ask an owner or a mechanic rather than trusting a rule.

Should I compare total cost of ownership instead?

Yes if you are buying. Fuel is one line; depreciation and insurance are usually larger, and the ownership calculator covers them.

Fuel prices, efficiency and incentives

Electricity and gasoline prices move. The per-mile energy use should come from the window sticker or FuelEconomy.gov, not from a category average.
