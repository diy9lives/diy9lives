What these are for

A calculator answers one question with the numbers you give it. A guide answers the question that comes next: which number should you have given it, and what does the result actually change?

Each guide below picks a decision — a paycheck, a car deal, an hourly rate, a house, a weekend project, a solar quote — and works a real example through the tools on this site. The arithmetic is shown, not summarised. Where a figure comes from an agency or a manufacturer, it is linked with the year, so you can check it against the source instead of trusting the page.

Read the guide first if you are still forming the question. Go straight to the calculator if you already know your inputs and only want the number. Each guide links to the tools it uses, and each tool links back to the guide.

How the numbers are checked

The worked examples are computed from the same formulas the calculators run, so you can reproduce any of them by hand. External figures — tax brackets, wage bases, bag yields, sizing charts — keep the citation and the tax or publication year on the page where they are used. Assumptions that belong to this site, like a 20 square foot allowance for a door, are labelled as assumptions.

If a figure looks wrong, the contact page is the place to say so. Corrections change the date at the bottom of the page, and the date only moves when the content does.

These are estimates for planning. They are not tax, legal, financial, or medical advice, and the terms say so at more length.
