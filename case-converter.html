How it works

Type or paste text and click a style. Uppercase and lowercase change every letter. Title Case capitalizes each word except small words like “and,” “of,” and “the” in the middle. Sentence case capitalizes the first letter of each sentence. camelCase removes spaces and capitalizes each word after the first, and snake_case lowercases everything and joins words with underscores.

Frequently asked questions

What is camelCase and when do I use it?

It is a naming style for programming, like userFirstName, where words are joined with no spaces and each word after the first starts with a capital letter.

What is snake_case?

A naming style where words are lowercase and separated by underscores, such as user_first_name. It is common in databases and some programming languages.

Why are some small words not capitalized in Title Case?

Many style guides leave short words like “a,” “the,” and “of” lowercase in the middle of a title. The first and last words are always capitalized.

Does it change my original text?

No. Your input stays as it is, and the converted version appears in the result box for you to copy.

Using the number

“a tale of two cities” in this page’s title case becomes “A Tale of Two Cities.” “of” stays lower because it is on the small-word list and it is not the first or last word. “A Tale Of Two Cities” is what you get from a converter that capitalizes every word. Style guides disagree on the list. The Chicago Manual and the Associated Press do not publish the same exceptions, and neither one is encoded here. If a house style says to capitalize “of,” do it yourself.

camelCase and snake_case are for identifiers, not for prose. “two cities” becomes “twoCities” or “two_cities.” Punctuation in the middle of a name is not handled with any special care. The original text stays in the box. Only the output changes.
