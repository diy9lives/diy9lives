How it works

For two dates, total days is the difference between them, plus one if you check inclusive. The years, months, and days breakdown uses calendar months, borrowing days from the previous month when needed. Business days counts Monday through Friday between the dates, and weekend days counts Saturdays and Sundays. The second card adds or subtracts days, weeks, months, or years from a start date, and when a month is too short it lands on that month's last day.

Frequently asked questions

Are public holidays excluded from business days?

No. Only weekends are excluded, so subtract any public holidays that fall on weekdays yourself.

What does inclusive mean?

Inclusive counts both the start and end dates. From Monday to Tuesday is 1 day normally, or 2 days if you count both.

What happens when I add a month to January 31?

February has no 31st, so the result is the last day of February. The tool never skips ahead into March.

Which time zone does it use?

Dates are whole calendar days in your own browser's time zone, with no times of day involved.

Using the number

Monday January 5, 2026 through Friday January 16, 2026 is 12 calendar days if you count both ends, and 10 weekdays. New Year’s Day that year fell on Thursday the 1st, so it is outside this span. Put a federal holiday inside the span and this page still counts it as a business day. It has no holiday table.

That is the right behavior for a personal count and the wrong behavior for a court deadline or a payroll period. Subtract the holidays yourself. OPM publishes the federal list. Your state and your employer will have others. Adding one month to January 31 lands on the last day of February, which the FAQ already explains. The time-zone page is the one that knows about offsets. This one uses the calendar dates you type and ignores the clock.
