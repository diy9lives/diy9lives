⚠ Know the reality

The house always has an edge. Blackjack: ~0.5% edge (best odds in the casino). Roulette: 5.26%. Slots: 2-15%. Sports betting: 4-6% average vig.


A negative expected value means you will lose money over time — it is mathematically guaranteed with enough bets. The calculator above shows you exactly how much.


Set a hard budget before you play. Never chase losses. Gambling should be entertainment, not income.


If gambling is causing problems: National Council on Problem Gambling | 1-800-522-4700

How it works

Expected value per bet is your win probability × the payout if you win, minus your loss probability × your stake. House edge is the negative of that value as a share of the stake. Break-even odds are the win probability at which expected value is zero. The session projection multiplies expected value by the number of bets. The casino game presets fill in standard published house edges for a single bet at typical rules, and the odds converter switches between American and decimal formats.

Frequently asked questions

Can I beat the house in the long run?

Not with a negative expected value bet. Short-term results swing widely, so you can win in a session, but the average loss per bet tends toward the house edge over many bets.

What is house edge?

The average share of each bet the casino expects to keep over time. A 5% edge means about $5 lost on average for every $100 wagered.

Are the preset edges exact for my casino?

They are standard published figures for common rules. Real edges vary with table rules, the number of decks, payouts, and, for blackjack, how closely you follow basic strategy. Slots vary widely by machine.

Where can I get help with gambling problems?

In the US, the National Problem Gambling Helpline is 1-800-GAMBLER, and it also offers online chat. Outside the US, search for your country's gambling support service.
