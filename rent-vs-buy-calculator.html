How it works

The buying side adds up everything you pay over your time horizon: the down payment, mortgage payments, property tax and maintenance (both a percentage of the home's value as it appreciates), insurance that rises about 2% a year, about 3% of the price in closing costs, and about 6% of the future value in selling costs. From that it subtracts the equity you own at the end, which is the home's future value minus your remaining loan balance.

The renting side adds up your rent, which grows by your annual increase, and subtracts the investment gain you would earn by investing the down payment instead. Whichever net cost is lower wins.

Frequently asked questions

What does this leave out?

Mortgage interest and property tax deductions, mortgage insurance, HOA dues, moving costs, and the value of stability or flexibility. It also assumes steady appreciation and investment returns, which real markets do not deliver.

How long do I need to stay for buying to win?

Try different values for the years you will stay. Buying costs are heavily front-loaded by closing costs and early interest, so shorter stays often favor renting and longer stays favor buying.

Why does the investment return matter?

Money used for a down payment could be invested instead. A higher assumed return makes renting more attractive, and a lower one makes buying more attractive.

Is renting “throwing money away”?

Not necessarily. Owners also pay interest, taxes, insurance, and upkeep that they never get back. The calculator shows which path costs less on your numbers.
