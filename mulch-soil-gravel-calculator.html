How it works

Volume in cubic feet is the area times depth in inches ÷ 12, increased by your extra percentage. Dividing by 27 gives cubic yards, and dividing by your bag size gives the number of bags. Approximate weight multiplies cubic yards by a typical weight per yard: about 800 lb for mulch, 1,200 for compost, 2,100 for topsoil, 2,700 for sand, 2,800 for gravel, and 2,600 for pea gravel or river rock. Choosing a material also sets a typical starting depth.

Frequently asked questions

How deep should I spread each material?

Two to three inches of mulch around plants, about four inches of topsoil for new lawn areas, and two to three inches of gravel for paths are common starting points.

How accurate are the weights?

They are rough averages. Moisture changes weight a lot, especially for mulch, compost, and soil. If you order by the ton, ask your supplier for their figure.

Is buying in bulk cheaper than bags?

Usually, for larger areas. A cubic yard equals 13.5 bags of 2 cubic feet, so bulk delivery tends to cost less once you need a few yards.

Can I enter an irregular area?

Yes. Enter the total square footage in the area box and it will be used instead of length and width.

Using the number

A 20 by 8 foot bed is 160 square feet. At 3 inches, plus the 5% extra this page starts with, that is 42 cubic feet, or about 1.56 cubic yards. In 2 cubic foot bags that is 21 bags. One cubic yard is 27 cubic feet, so a single bulk yard covers most of this bed and a second yard covers it with mulch left over. Bulk is usually cheaper once you are past one yard, and only if a truck can reach the bed.

The ton line multiplies yards by a dry-ish weight: about 800 pounds per yard for mulch, so this bed is a bit over half a ton. Wet mulch can weigh much more. If a landscape yard sells by the ton, ask them what their mulch weighs this week instead of trusting 800. Gravel at about 2,800 pounds per yard is a different truck. Do not use the mulch weight for a driveway. Depths in the FAQ, two to three inches of mulch and about four of topsoil, are starting points, not a soil test.
