From a room list to boxes

  1. Add each room with its length and width. The page totals the area, so an L-shaped room should be split into rectangles.
  2. Set the waste percentage. Five percent suits a simple straight lay; 10% is safer for doorways, angles and a first installation.
  3. Enter the square feet per box and the price per square foot. The box count rounds up, because you cannot buy a partial box.
  4. Read the spare square footage. Keep one extra box for a repair; a discontinued lot is hard to match later.

How to use this calculator

Flooring is sold by the box, and boxes cover a stated area, so this calculator exists to get you off square footage and onto a real purchase order.

  1. Measure each room's length and width and add it as its own row. Include closets and the strip of hallway that gets the same floor.
  2. Read the square feet per box off the carton, not the brand's website. It is usually printed near the coverage line and it varies from 15 to 30 square feet.
  3. Choose a waste percentage that matches the layout: about 10% for straight runs, 15% for diagonal, 20% or more for herringbone and chevron patterns.
  4. Enter the price per square foot from the quote. The estimate is boxes times the coverage in each box, so it reflects what you actually pay.
  5. Read the box count, not the square footage. That is the number the store will hand you.

A worked example: a living room and a hallway

A 16 by 20 living room is 320 square feet. The hallway is 3 by 12, or 36. Together, 356 square feet. Ten percent waste brings it to 391.6 square feet.

A box covering 24.3 square feet means 391.6 ÷ 24.3, or 16.1 boxes, which rounds to 17 boxes. Those 17 boxes contain 413.1 square feet, so you are buying about 57 square feet more than the room measures. At $4.29 a square foot that is $1,772.20, and it is not the same as 356 × $4.29.

Change one assumption and watch the order move. Run the same floor in a herringbone pattern at 20% waste and you need 427.2 square feet, which is 17.6 boxes, so 18 boxes. One extra box, roughly $104, is the price of the pattern. That is usually worth paying, and it is exactly the kind of number a quote leaves out.

What your results mean

The gap between the square footage you need and the square footage you buy is not an error. It is the difference between a floor and a floor plus the offcuts, and every cut in a doorway or around a vent comes out of a full plank.

Keep the spare box. Plank and tile flooring is made in dye lots, and a replacement box bought two years later will very likely be a slightly different shade. The leftover box is also the only way to repair a dropped-pan dent without redoing a room.

The cost figure covers flooring only. Underlayment, transitions and thresholds, quarter round, adhesive, a tapping block, and any labor are all outside it, and on a 356 square foot job those add up faster than the waste percentage does. Get the installed number before you compare a contractor's bid against your own estimate.

Acclimation is the step that ruins more installs than arithmetic does. Most manufacturers want the cartons in the room at normal temperature for a stated period before installation, because wood and laminate expand and contract with humidity. Check the carton for that number and follow it.

Frequently asked questions

How much waste should I allow?

Ten percent for a straight lay in simple rectangular rooms, 15% for diagonal, and 20% or more for herringbone and chevron. Add a little more for rooms with many corners, angled walls, or several doorways.

Should I buy an extra box on purpose?

Yes, one. It covers a miscount, a damaged plank, and the dye-lot problem later. On a large job two spare boxes is not unreasonable, and the cost is a fraction of a percent.

Do closets and hallways count?

Only if they get the new floor. Add each as its own room so its area is included once. A closet you are leaving carpeted should be left out entirely.

How many square feet are in a box?

It varies by product, from about 15 to 30 square feet, and it is printed on the carton. This calculator cannot guess it, which is why you enter it.

Does the price include installation?

No. It is boxes times coverage times your price per square foot. Labor, underlayment, trim, and adhesive are separate, and installation is usually the larger half of a flooring bill.

What about the stairs?

Stairs are measured differently, by the tread and riser of each step. For a rough allowance, treat each step as roughly 6 square feet of material with waste, then confirm with your installer before ordering.

What the flooring estimate cannot see

Waste depends on the layout, not just the room shape. Diagonal runs, herringbone, many doorways and narrow last rows all raise the percentage. The calculator does not include underlayment, trim, thresholds or the cost of levelling a floor. Keep one extra box; a discontinued lot is much harder to match than a leftover box is to store.
